## How to run

```sh
uv run python -m flow run comp [--pdk P] [--instance I]          # characterize, into build/sweep/comp/<run>
uv run python -m flow layout comp                                 # lay out the newest run's instances
uv run python -m flow layout comp --instance frida1 [--auto]      # a named instance by all three tools
uv run python -m flow table comp                                  # the viewer's table
uv run python -m flow plot comp [FIGURE ...] --from DIR           # a run's or the lab's captures' figures
```

## Characterization

Each instance at the nominal common mode: the delay and energy at 0.5 mV and 100 mV, the delay over 0.1–100 mV (its metastability and regeneration time constant), the kickback (the inputs' largest excursion over a decision at 0.5 mV, driven as a DAC's top plates through 1 MΩ onto 1 pF), and the offset and noise from a transient-noise S-curve: 0 and 25 µV to 3 mV a side, 33 points, 80 decisions a point in passes through every input in a fresh random order, the Gaussian of most likelihood, the input driven through 1 Ω onto 100 fF. One noise reading scatters by about 4 % (one sigma) between seeds; the 16 %/84 % crossings of 40 decisions a point, each input held, scattered by about 14 %. The S-curve again over the common mode, the delay and energy over the supply. One clock: 1 ns of reset, then 4 ns of evaluation (the converter allows about 2 ns), checked by the noise-free decisions over the input against twice it: every decision resolved, the same decisions, the delays within 10 %, 10 µV resolved within the evaluation; a comparator that does not fails, its record holding why.

On Spectre's own steps (no strobe, no bound on the step) three TSMC65 comparators' delays came 1 to 6 % short and their energy per decision 1.3 to 2.7 times too large against a 1 ps step, for a quarter less run time and half the raw size: the runs keep the 2 ps strobe (delays within 0.1 % of the 1 ps step's; the energy at 0.5 mV still 8 % above it).

## Design space

Each family is a function of a few knobs (`flow/comp/sweep.py`), swept over the product of their values; the runner keeps in each PDK what `params.valid` admits there. Every width times `k` divides the noise by about √k (toward a floor) and multiplies the energy by `k`, keeping the delay.

- `reference(topology, size)`: the StrongARM and the double tail at the characterization's short-channel sizes, every key width ×2 and ×4, the input pair or the latch alone ×2, the input pair and tail twice as long. The triple tail, the self-timed double tails and the modified double tail at these sizes were dominated (another instance as quiet, as fast and as frugal) in TSMC65 and dominated or too slow in the older PDKs; they stay named, not swept.
- `frida1_like(k)`: the StrongARM near FRIDA-1's hand-sized comparator in round sizes, ×1 and ×2 (its lengths doubled too decided in 7 to 35 ns).
- `base_strongarm(tail_l, k)`: the base StrongARM with every width ×`k` but its tail, 4 k units at 14, 34 or 54 minimum lengths: about 0.5, 1 and 1.5 ns in TSMC65.
- `dynamic_bias(ratio, k)`: the modified double tail's latch, the input pair's tail a minimum-length switch onto C_TAIL, which stops the pair once its drains, loaded by C_P, have fallen by a fraction C_TAIL/C_P sets: the pair integrates in weak inversion and spends only that charge. C_TAIL/C_P of 6, 3.5, 2.75 and 2.25: about 0.75, 1, 1.35 and 1.95 ns in TSMC65.
- `frida1`: FRIDA-1's fabricated comparator, named; `c040` and the characterization's six topologies, named but not swept, for their layouts.

Per PDK (`params.LIMITS`): FRIDA-1's latch lengths decide in time only in TSMC65 and TSMC28; the 54-unit tail only in TSMC65 and TPSC65, the 34-unit tail neither in Tower180 nor in TSMC28 (beyond its models' lengths), and in Tower180 no tail beyond 4; the dynamic bias from 2.75 in TPSC65, at 6 in LF150, up to 2.75 in TSMC28, not in IHP130 or Tower180. The limits keep 15 % to spare: 10 µV at the nominal supply and 0.5 mV at 90 % of it within 3.4 ns of the 4 ns evaluation.

In TSMC65 the dynamic bias reaches an energy times noise power of about 4.5 fJ mV² (about 160 µV/√k at 170 k fJ), where the base StrongARM takes 9 to 13 and FRIDA-1's comparator scaled 16; fitted as √(a²/k + f²) each family is within 2 to 6 %. The viewer's scatter of `noise_v` against `energy_0p5mv_j` from a run shows the same. The ADC study's comparator grid (`flow/adc/sweep.py`: `COMP_GRID`) takes from these, for each of five noise levels by four delays, the instance of least energy within 15 % of the noise and 20 % of the delay.
