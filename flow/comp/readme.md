## How to run

```sh
uv run python -m flow run comp [--pdk P] [--instance I]          # characterize, into build/sweep/comp/<run>
uv run python -m flow layout comp                                 # lay out the newest run's instances
uv run python -m flow layout comp --instance frida1 [--auto]      # a named instance by all three tools
uv run python -m flow table comp                                  # the viewer's table
uv run python -m flow plot comp [FIGURE ...] --from DIR           # a run's or the lab's captures' figures
```

## Characterization

Each instance at the nominal common mode: the delay and energy at 0.5 mV and 100 mV, the delay over 0.1–100 mV (its metastability and regeneration time constant), the kickback (the inputs' largest excursion over a decision at 0.5 mV, driven as a DAC's top plates through 1 MΩ onto 1 pF), and the offset and noise from a transient-noise S-curve: 0 and 25 µV to 3 mV a side, 33 points, 80 decisions a point in passes through every input in a fresh random order, the Gaussian of most likelihood, the input driven through 1 Ω onto 100 fF. One noise reading scatters by about 4 % (one sigma) between seeds; the 16 %/84 % crossings of 40 decisions a point, each input held, scattered by about 14 %. The S-curve again over the common mode, the delay and energy over the supply. One clock: 1 ns of reset, then 4 ns of evaluation (the converter allows about 2 ns), checked by the noise-free decisions over the input against twice it: every decision resolved, the same decisions, the delays within 10 %, 10 µV resolved within the evaluation; a comparator that does not fails, its record holding why.

The energy per decision is the supply's charge over a decision's cycle, read off a charge meter the simulator integrates (the supply source's current mirrored onto 1 pF, `params.CHARGE_METER`), exact at any output step, at the supply's voltage, averaged over the decisions that follow one at the same input. A run's first decision starts from the operating point and costs 1.1 to 3.5 times a settled one, moving by up to 30 % with the step; left out, the energy agrees within 0.3 % between a 0.5, 1, 2 and 10 ps strobe on three TSMC65 comparators. The records that averaged the first decision in overstate the energy 1.0 to 1.9 times, the base StrongARM's the most.

On Spectre's own steps (no strobe, no bound on the step) the same comparators' delays came 1 to 6 % short and their settled energy up to 12 % off, for a quarter less run time and half the raw size: the runs keep the 2 ps strobe (delays within 0.1 % of a 1 ps step's).

## Design space

Each family is a function of a few knobs (`flow/comp/sweep.py`), swept over products of their values, each product in the PDKs it names; the runner keeps in each PDK what `params.valid` admits there. Every width times `k` divides the noise by about √k (toward a floor) and multiplies the energy by `k`, keeping the delay. Each PDK's products fill a grid of five noise levels (200, 150, 100, 70, 50 µV) by four delays at 0.5 mV (0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 ns), each cell with the more frugal family there, sized from the families' fits (√(a²/k + f²)), refitted once on the instances' own characterization: 23 instances in TSMC65, 7 in TSMC28, 20 in TPSC65, IHP130 and Tower180, 16 in LF150, whose StrongARM's noise floor (45 to 60 µV) leaves its 50 µV row empty.

- `base_strongarm(tail_l, k)`: the base StrongARM with every width ×`k` but its tail, 4 k units `tail_l` minimum lengths long: the tail's length sets the delay. TSMC65 at 14 (0.5 ns); TPSC65 at 6, 16, 26 and 36; IHP130 at 4, 11, 20 and 28; LF150 at 4, 13, 23 and 34; Tower180 at 3, 9, 15 and 21; TSMC28 at TSMC65's physical sizes (three of its 40 nm units to one of 120 nm, the tail 840 nm long, about 0.47 ns) for 200 to 70 µV, its 50 µV cell (k of about 330) and its slower columns left empty.
- `dynamic_bias(ratio, k)`: the modified double tail's latch, the input pair's tail a minimum-length switch onto C_TAIL, which stops the pair once its drains, loaded by C_P, have fallen by a fraction C_TAIL/C_P sets: the pair integrates in weak inversion and spends only that charge. TSMC65 at C_TAIL/C_P of 3.5, 2.75 and 2.25 (1, 1.35, 1.9 ns); TPSC65 at 3.5 and 2.75; LF150 at 6 for its 100 and 70 µV cells at 1 ns.
- `reference(topology)`: the StrongARM and the double tail at the characterization's short-channel sizes, in TSMC65 and TSMC28.
- `frida1`: FRIDA-1's fabricated comparator; the characterization's six topologies, named but not swept, for their layouts (`c040`, the retired size sweep's topology 40, retired with it).

Per PDK (`params.LIMITS`): FRIDA-1's latch lengths decide in time only in TSMC65 and TSMC28; the 54-unit tail not in LF150 and IHP130, the 34-unit tail not in TSMC28 (beyond its models' lengths); the dynamic bias from 2.75 in TPSC65, at 6 in LF150, up to 2.75 in TSMC28, up to 3.5 in Tower180, not in IHP130. The limits keep 15 % to spare: 10 µV at the nominal supply and 0.5 mV at 90 % of it within 3.4 ns of the 4 ns evaluation. Tower180, recalibrated after its devices' finger widths were fixed (13 instances), decides in time at every tail up to 14 (1.4 ns); its dynamic bias at 3.5 is no more frugal than the StrongARM, at 6 it misses its clock.

In TSMC65 the energy times noise power of the dynamic bias is 3.9 to 4.3 fJ mV², of the base StrongARM 5.8 to 7.5 (counting settled decisions; the records that counted the first decision too gave the StrongARM twice that). The pruned topologies (the triple tail, the self-timed double tails, the modified double tail at these sizes, FRIDA-1's comparator scaled) were dominated (another instance as quiet, as fast and as frugal) in TSMC65 and dominated or too slow in the older PDKs. The ADC study's comparator grid (`flow/adc/sweep.py`: `COMP_GRID`) is TSMC65's grid, FRIDA-1's at 200 µV and 2 ns.
