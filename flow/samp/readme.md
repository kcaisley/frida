## How to run

```sh
uv run python -m flow --block samp --run scaled --to results                # characterize a grid
uv run python -m flow --block samp --run scaled --from layout --to pex     # lay its designs out, signed off
uv run python -m flow --block samp --run frida1 --to lvs                   # one switch by ALIGN, without extraction
uv run python -m flow --block samp --run scaled --from table --to table    # the viewer's table
uv run python -m flow --block samp --run scaled --to results --dry-run      # what would run
uv run python -m flow --block samp --run frida1 --to postsim                # post-layout, back-annotated
```

## Design space

- `scaled`: each switch 8 to 128 units wide, a quarter to four times FRIDA-1's (`scaled(tgate, mos_w=32, dummy=False)` is FRIDA-1's transmission gate; `tgate` with and without half-size dummies, `nmos`, `pmos`, and the bootstrapped switch by width from 32 units, `scaled(bootstrap_w64)`, its helpers an eighth, none under 4 units, the finest PDK's minimum width, its boost capacitor and its bottom-plate switch's length sized together for the bench's 1 pF or a smaller load: the capacitor twelve times what it shares its charge with, every device on the boosted nodes counted from the netlist, so that V_b' stays above 0.9 V_DD with a third again for the wells and the wiring (Razavi-ADC Eq. (3.70)), the bottom-plate switch the shortest at which the bottom plate cannot pass the output; a switch under 32 units would need a bottom-plate switch with over twice its own gate and is left out).
- `long_channel`: each switch (the PMOS switch too) at twice its length.
- `boost_ladder`: the bootstrapped switch as an on-resistance ladder, the ADC study's samplers (`flow.adc.runs.converters`), each rung by its width: `boost_ladder(w32)`, `w64`, `w112` and `w160`, each sized as `scaled`'s, so every rung is boosted alike.
- `frida1`, `tgate_dummy`, `nmos`, `bootstrap`: the four switches by name, for their layouts (each its hand-written floorplan), not swept apart from their points in `scaled`.

The bootstrap's PMOS n-wells sit on the boosted top plate in the netlist; the layout's row cells put every well on vdd, so its layout needs a separate n-well (flow/layout) before LVS matches.

A bootstrapped switch's gate rises to V_b' above its input as tracking starts, whatever its output: had it risen at once, an output far from the input (a sample far from the previous one) would put V_b' and that distance across the switch's oxide at its output end until the output follows. The gate rides V_b' above the capacitor's bottom plate, so the bottom-plate switch is made long enough that the bottom plate, rising from 0 V, cannot pass the output (where the two meet, the two switches have the same terminal voltages and the long one the smaller current into its node): the gate then stands no more than V_b' above the output. The cost is a slower start, which the acquisition times hold, and a bootstrapping path that follows the input only far below its own corner frequency (Razavi-ADC Eq. (3.79), (3.80)); the on-resistance is the closed switch's, read after a step late in the track phase, once the gate drive has settled.

Gate-drive settling. Every kit's bootstrap is sized with the rough 65-nm unit capacitances (`params.py`), so in a kit with larger unit capacitances its bottom-plate switch comes out longer than its own rule asks and its gate drive settles later after tracking starts: in IHP130 to within 2 % of its track-end value up to 6 ns after the clock's rise in the 32-unit switch (the slow corner) and up to 10 ns in the 32 x 2 one (hot). The level runs give it 15 ns before their step; a level whose gate drive has not settled by then has no time constant (NaN), and the supply and corner maxima are NaN where any of their runs has none. Kit-specific unit capacitances belong in each kit (pdk/), which holds none yet.

Calibrations. The simulated values the comments of `params.py` and `model.py` point here for, in TSMC65 (typical, 25 C unless said):

- The device constants (`W_MIN_UM` to `PMOS_K_A_PER_V2`, `Model.Behavioral`'s 0.35 V thresholds): a 65-nm switch's triode conductance a volt is about a tenth below k' W / L, its threshold rises 0.1 to 0.15 V with its source above the bulk, and its source and drain resistances add about a quarter to R_on; the on-resistance the constants give without a measured curve is 30 to 65 % below a simulated switch's, about half.
- The unit capacitances (`GATE_F` to `BOTTOM_F`): within 5 % of the simulated gates and overlaps, gates one to sixteen units long; a junction's and the channel-to-substrate's capacitance up to two fifths above their mean at 0 V and a third below it at the supply. The bottom plate's count (`_bottom_f`) a fifth low at 0 V and a tenth high at the supply against Spectre's, and a tenth low in a falling step.
- `BOTTOM_LIFT`: a minimum-length switch carries 2.2 to 3.0 times less current a square than the 32-unit design's bottom-plate switch (13 units long) at one bias over the supply, the corners and the temperature (DC); the constant is the upper end.
- kT/C (`Model.behavioral`): a noise run reads about a tenth below sqrt(kT / C), the device model's channel noise in deep triode below 4kT g_ds.
- The model's residue (`Model.sample`): a full-scale step's, where the track window is under about ten time constants, is up to five times exp(-T / RC).

Stress. The limit is the kit's nominal supply times its largest corner (110 %): at the highest supply it is the supply itself. Every run saves every device's values, and `terminal_max_v` is the largest pair over all runs but the DAC's (`terminal_max_dac_v`). The full-scale step runs at the nominal and at the highest supply, the converter's own worst case (the output a full scale from the input as tracking starts, the input moving there no faster than a full-scale sine at Nyquist), and the level runs' input moves no faster while the switch holds (its step in the track phase, the switch closed, is a clock edge's: the step response the time constant is read from). Under it the switch's gate stands no more than V_b' above its output, and no device of the pull-down stack has more than a supply across it.

At the nominal supply every terminal pair of every device stays within the limit in every run of every bootstrapped size, the complementary clock an edge late included (TSMC65 and IHP130: at most 1.263 V, in the skew run).

At the highest supply the limit is not met, and it is open (no change here meets it):

- The runs there take clock edges that meet. With the complement an edge late, the top-plate switch's gate control node floats from the clock's rise to the complement's fall, and its pull-up's turn-off lifts it 60 to 67 mV above the supply: 1.379 V in TSMC65 (32 units), 1.385 to 1.386 V in IHP130 (32 units and 32 x 2). The converter's clocks must therefore meet at its highest supply, the complement falling no later than the clock rises; flow.adc's digital block makes the complement and states no such bound yet.
- With edges that meet, the pairs that hold a whole supply while the switch holds pass it by what couples onto a node held at a rail (TSMC65 and IHP130, every bootstrapped size). As tracking starts the bottom-plate switch's turn-off kicks the bottom plate below 0 V while the precharge device holds the top plate at the supply: the boost capacitor up to 0.89 mV past in TSMC65 and 1.43 mV in IHP130, and the gate, which follows the top plate through the precharge device's channel, up to 0.43 and 0.80 mV below 0 V. In IHP130, as holding starts, the complementary clock lifts the top-plate switch's gate control node above the supply until the clock has fallen (up to 3.7 mV, the 32 x 2 switch). While the input falls at the converter's rate the gate, held at 0 V through the pull-down's channels, follows it through the switch's overlap (0.065 mV in TSMC65, 0.16 mV in IHP130). The record's `terminal_max_v` reads that much past the limit at the highest supply.
- No sizing removes these. A node held at a rail through a channel is moved past the rail by every coupling outward from it, and at the highest supply a pair between that node and the opposite rail has no margin; the falling input's R C dV/dt on the gate is there for any finite resistance of the pull-down. Only nodes held inside the rails (the off gate a set margin above 0 V, at the cost of the off switch's leakage) or a limit with room for these couplings would leave no pair past it.

`gate_bulk_max_v` (the boosted gate over the substrate, up to two supplies) is kept apart: under an inverted channel the oxide carries V_GS.
